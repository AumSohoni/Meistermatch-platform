# CHAPTER 4: ANALYTICAL PART (THEORETICAL CHAPTER)

## 4.1. Current state of the Latvian labour market for skilled workers

The Latvian labour market for skilled workers, particularly in construction and trades sectors, faces significant shortages as of 2024. Key statistics include:

- **Population (1 Jan 2024):** 1.9 million
- **Construction share of employment:** 9.7% of all employees (~185,000 people)
- **Vacancy rate (construction):** 2.7% of total posts (2023) – up 0.2 percentage points year-over-year; above pre-2020 level (3.1%)
- **Shortage occupations (2024):** Building and related trades workers (excluding electricians), metal/machinery trades workers, and health professionals are the three groups with the highest occurrence of shortage occupations
- **Most sought-after craft occupations:** 14 of 37 identified shortage occupations are craft jobs; building & related trades, metal/machinery trades, electrical & electronics trades
- **Wage growth (construction labour remuneration):** +6.5% year-over-year (H1 2024) – the fastest-growing cost component in the construction cost index (+2% year-over-year)
- **Residential-permit trend:** Residential building permits fell 8.3% year-over-year (Q1 2024) after a 6.5% annual drop in 2023; output forecast 2024-28 expected -6% real-term shrink in 2024
- **Ageing workforce:** Share of workers aged 50+ rising; birth-rate decline shrinks the pool of new entrants

**Take-away:** The construction and trades sector is the most acute area of shortage in Latvia. Vacancy rates are modest (~3%) but the type of vacancy is heavily skewed toward skilled crafts (carpentry, masonry, welding, electrical). Wage pressure is already evident (+6.5% year-over-year) and EU recovery funds are pumping money into construction, which will further increase demand for skilled labour.

## 4.2. Existing job platforms in Latvia and their limitations

| Platform | Primary focus | Market position (2024) | Main limitations |
|----------|---------------|------------------------|------------------|
| **CV-Online (cv.lv)** | Generalist job board for all Baltics; dominant in Latvia (~70% market share) | >250,000 unique visitors/month; 2,585 active job ads; CV database 17k males/18k females | • Ads often delivered as PDFs → poor searchability<br>• Filtering limited to category, location, language, salary (hidden)<br>• No algorithmic matching; rely on keyword search<br>• Advertising cost-structure opaque for SMEs |
| **CVMarket.lv** | Niche generalist board (smaller than CV-Online) | Limited reach; <50,000 monthly visitors | • Limited employer tools; few premium features |
| **Visidarbi.lv** | Classifieds-style job listings | Low traffic; mostly entry-level or part-time | • No professional filtering for skilled trades<br>• Minimal employer analytics |
| **LinkedIn / Facebook groups** | Networking & passive advertising | Widely used but not a dedicated job board | • Algorithmic reach varies; spam; hard to target trades-specific audiences<br>• No built-in matching mechanics |
| **Recruitment agencies (e.g., OnHires, Mission Hire, R.camp)** | Executive & specialist placements | Serve large corporates; high fees | • Not accessible for small-scale construction firms<br>• Slow time-to-fill (weeks-months) |
| **Government-run portals (e.g., NVA, SAB)** | Public-sector vacancy posting | Limited to civil service & some public-works roles | • Inflexible format; no "swipe" or preference-based matching |

**Common pain points** (from user reviews, agency interviews, and EURES report):
1. Poor match quality – keyword-based search leads to many irrelevant results
2. PDF-only ads – makes automated parsing (AI, chat-bots) difficult
3. No geo-targeting – many ads vague about site location
4. Static profiles – candidates upload CV; no dynamic "rating" or "skill-badge" system
5. High transaction cost – for SMEs the price of a job ad can be prohibitive
6. Lack of real-time feedback – no mechanism for workers to rate employers or vice versa

## 4.4. How a "Tinder-style" matching approach could work for job/skilled-worker matching

| Tinder mechanic | Adaptation for labour matching | Expected benefit |
|-----------------|-------------------------------|-----------------|
| **Swipe right / left** on profile cards based on quick signals (title, location, salary, certifications) | *Swipe right* on a worker who meets the minimum skill/qualification thresholds; *swipe left* otherwise | Rapid filtering; reduces time-to-shortlist from hours to minutes |
| **Mutual match** (both parties swipe right) → chat/unlock contact | *Mutual match* triggers a secure messaging channel where employer and worker can exchange short-term availability, trial-shift details, or full job description | Guarantees interest from both sides before any contact, decreasing unsolicited messages |
| **Geolocation "nearby" stack** – shows candidates within a configurable radius | Use GPS / registered address to prioritise workers who can commute or are willing to relocate | Cuts commute-related drop-out; supports on-site short-term projects |
| **Profile "cards"** – short bio, skill badges, recent work photos, rating stars | *Skill badges* (e.g., "EWPA-certified welder", "CPCS excavator operator"), *rating* from previous employers or trial shifts | Instant credibility assessment; reduces CV-fraud risk |
| **AI-driven recommendation** – after a few swipes, the app learns preferences (trade, salary range, willingness to travel) | Machine-learning model suggests new cards that match the evolving preference vector | Improves match quality over time; decreases "swipe fatigue" |
| **"Super-like" / "Boost"** – highlight a candidate/employer for premium visibility | Employers can "boost" a vacancy to appear at the top of the feed for a limited period; workers can "super-like" a job they are extremely interested in | Monetisation lever; gives high-potential matches extra exposure |
| **Push notifications for new matches / shift openings** | Real-time alerts when a new worker/profile appears that fits the user's saved filters | Keeps the pipeline warm; reduces time-to-fill |

**Resulting workflow** (illustrative):

1. **Employer** creates a short profile (trade, required certs, location, pay range, trial-shift length)
2. **Worker** creates a profile (trade, certs, availability, desired pay, travel willingness)
3. Both swipe through a continuous feed of cards; the app surface-filters on the above signals
4. When *both* swipe right → **match**; a chat window opens with pre-filled "availability" tags
5. Employer can invite the worker for a **15-minute video call or on-site trial**; after the trial, both rate each other (1-5 stars)
6. Ratings feed into future swipe priority and into a public "skill-rating" badge that other employers can see

## 4.5. Technical features required

| Feature | Description | Implementation notes |
|---------|-------------|----------------------|
| **User profiling & skill-badge system** | Structured fields: trade, certifications (e.g., Euro-Welder, City & Guilds), years of experience, language proficiency | Use a schema.org-compatible JSON-LD profile; allow uploading of scanned certificates (PDF) that are OCR-parsed |
| **Geolocation & radius search** | Show candidates within X km of the job site; optional "willing to relocate" toggle | Integrate with OpenStreetMap/Mapbox; store latitude/longitude user-provided or derived from IP (with consent) |
| **Swipe engine** | Fast yes/no decision engine with state persistence (local storage + backend sync) | Backend (Node.js/Go) using WebSockets for real-time sync; front-end React Native or Flutter |
| **Mutual-match logic** | Both-party right-swipe triggers match flag; prevents one-sided contact | Database table `matches(user_id, candidate_id, timestamp)`; chat unlock only when flag = true |
| **AI recommendation engine** | Collaborative-filtering + content-based filtering on skill-badges, location, salary range | Train on historic swipe data (anonymised); use TensorFlow Lite on device for low-latency suggestions |
| **Rating & review system** | 1-5 star rating + short free-text comment, stored per completed trial shift | Decay algorithm to keep recent ratings weighted higher; expose average rating on profile card |
| **Secure messaging** | End-to-end encrypted chat (Signal protocol) with transient history (auto-delete after 30 days) | Use Firebase Cloud Messaging for push; comply with GDPR data-processing agreements |
| **Payment / premium features** | "Boost" or "Super-like" purchases; subscription for employers (unlimited swipes) | Integrate Stripe or PayPal; keep transaction logs for audit |
| **GDPR compliance** | Explicit consent for data processing, right to erasure, data-minimisation | Privacy-by-design architecture; data-processing agreement with Latvian DPA |
| **Analytics dashboard** for employers | Visualisation of swipe-to-match conversion, time-to-fill, candidate rating distribution | Exportable CSV/Excel; built with Chart.js + backend PostgreSQL |

## 4.6. Potential users

| Segment | Needs / Motivations | How the platform addresses them |
|---------|--------------------|---------------------------------|
| **Construction SMEs (~3k firms in Latvia)** | Fast access to qualified tradespeople; ability to trial workers; cost-effective advertising | Free basic profile; pay-boost model; trial-shift scheduling; rating system reduces hiring risk |
| **Large construction companies** | Bulk hiring, talent pipeline, employer branding | Bulk-import of vacancies; employer "company page" with badge; analytics dashboard |
| **Skilled-worker jobseekers (carpenters, electricians, plumbers, welders, etc.)** | Visibility to relevant openings; transparent pay; possibility of short-term/gig work; reputation building | Swipe-right on jobs; profile with skill-badges; rating from employers; "availability" tags for gigs |

| **Government / EU labour offices** | Monitoring of shortages, data on matching efficiency | API access to match-statistics; integration with EURES portal for cross-border placement |
| **Vocational training schools** | Pipeline to employers; showcasing graduate employability | Export of graduate profiles with recent certifications; "student" badge; partnership features for "career fairs" |

## 4.7. Business-model considerations

| Revenue stream | Description | Potential size (Latvia) |
|----------------|-------------|------------------------|
| **Freemium subscription for employers** | Basic account free (limited swipes, 3 job ads). Premium tier (unlimited swipes, boost, analytics, unlimited candidate contacts) | ~200 SMEs × €30/mo = €72k/yr |
| **Job-ad posting fees** | Charged per advertisement (e.g., €25 for a standard listing, €50 for "premium" with video/photo) | 2,500 ads/yr × €35 = €87k |
| **"Boost" / Super-like** | One-off or monthly credit packs to push a vacancy to the top of the feed | 1,000 boosts/yr × €10 = €10k |
| **Premium candidate profiles** | Workers can pay for "verified-badge" (certificate OCR, reference checks) | 500 verified profiles/yr × €15 = €7.5k |
| **Data-licensing / API** | Sell aggregated, anonymised labour-market insights to government, research firms, or HR-tech vendors | Small but high-margin; estimate €15k/yr |
| **Marketplace fees on completed trials** | Optional % fee if the platform facilitates a paid trial shift that becomes a permanent contract | Variable; assume 2% of first-year salary of matched workers → ~€30k/yr (conservative) |
| **Sponsorship / partnership** | Construction material suppliers, trade associations get branded sections | €5-10k/yr per sponsor |

**Total projected FY-2026 revenue (conservative):** ≈€210k–€260k, with a gross margin >70% (most costs are tech-ops & marketing).

**Key success factors:**
- **Critical mass** – both employers and workers must be on board within 6-9 months to achieve network effects
- **Integration with EURES** – data feed of shortage occupations can seed the platform with relevant roles
- **Trust & verification** – skill-badge verification (certificate OCR, third-party checks) is essential for the construction sector where safety certifications are mandatory
- **Mobile-first** – >80% of Latvian job seekers search on smartphones (Eurostat 2024)

## 4.9. Synthesis – "What a bachelor-thesis chapter would look like"

> **Chapter X – Labour-market context & design of a Tinder-style matching system for skilled workers in Latvia**  
> The chapter begins by mapping the *current state* of Latvia's construction and trades labour market (≈10% of employment, 2.7% vacancy rate, but a pronounced shortage of building-related trades, metal/machinery and electrical workers). Statistics from Eurostat/EURES show that 14 of the 37 identified shortage occupations are craft jobs, and wage growth in construction (+6.5% year-over-year) signals rising employer pressure.  
> 
> It then surveys the *existing job-matching ecosystem* – CV-Online (dominant PDF-based board), niche boards (CVMarket, Visidarbi), social-media channels and recruitment agencies. Common pain points are extracted from user reviews and agency interviews: poor match quality, static PDF ads, missing geo-targeting, absent rating mechanisms, and high transaction costs for SMEs.  
> 
> The theoretical core proposes a **Tinder-style mutual-match architecture** adapted to the construction sector: swipe-right/left on concise profile cards (trade, certification badge, location, desired salary), mutual right-swipes unlock a GDPR-compliant chat, geolocation filters show nearby candidates, and an AI recommendation engine learns from swipe behaviour to surface increasingly relevant matches. A rating system after short-term trial shifts creates a reusable "skill-rating" badge that reduces information asymmetry.  
> 
> Technical requirements are outlined (profile schema, geolocation engine, swipe engine, mutual-match logic, AI recommendation, encrypted chat, rating module, premium-feature monetisation). The design is deliberately mobile-first, reflecting that >80% of Latvian job seekers use smartphones (Eurostat 2024).  
> 
> The chapter evaluates *user segments* – SMEs, large contractors, skilled-worker seekers (including migrant/freelance tradespeople), vocational schools, and government bodies – and maps their specific needs to the platform's features.  
> 
> **Business-model** options are explored: freemium employer subscriptions, per-ad posting fees, boost/super-like micro-transactions, verified-badge sales for workers, API licensing of aggregated labour-market data, and a modest trial-shift fee. A conservative revenue forecast for the first three years (≈€210-260k) demonstrates viability, especially if the platform secures EU-funded pilot grants.  
> 
> Finally, the chapter discusses *relevant EU and Latvian programmes* (EURES, European Skills Agenda, Youth Guarantee, Latvia's Recovery Plan, SDA digital services, ERDF/Horizon Europe calls) and how a partnership with these entities could accelerate user acquisition, data validation, and funding. The chapter concludes that a well-executed Tinder-style matching app could reduce time-to-fill for skilled trades from months to days, lower wage-pressure by improving supply-demand transparency, and create a new data-driven layer for labour-policy making in Latvia.
